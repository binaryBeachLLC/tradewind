3Description

The TW-1 is a complete, pole-mounted field sensor. It measures air temperature and humidity inside a louvred radiation shield, soil moisture and rainfall, and its own battery, solar and enclosure health. It reports over LoRaWAN every 5 minutes, or every minute in rapid mode, to any LoRaWAN 1.0.3 network server.

A linear charger with input-voltage regulation holds the 5 V panel near its maximum power point without an MPPT controller. A 75 nA buck-boost converter supplies the 3.3 V bus across the full Li-ion range. The panel mounts on a roof 25 mm above the white ASA enclosure and shades it, so the cell stays well inside its charging temperature range in tropical sun.

7Detailed Description

7.1Overview

The TW-1 combines a RAK3172 LoRaWAN module (STM32WLE5CC with a sub-GHz radio), a BQ24074 linear charger with power path, a TPS63900 buck-boost converter, a protected 18650 Li-ion cell and four field inputs on one 4-layer board inside a sealed ASA enclosure. Firmware runs on the module (RAKwireless RUI3). The device sleeps in STOP2 between scheduled uplinks and wakes early on rain-gauge pulses and magnet taps.

7.2Functional Block Diagram

7.3Feature Description

7.3.1 Solar Power Path

The charger runs in USB500 mode, so its input-voltage loop (VIN-DPM, 4.50 V typ) cuts the charge current before the panel collapses. A 5 V panel's maximum-power voltage falls to about 4.36 V at a working temperature of 55 °C, so this fixed point captures 99 % of the available power without an MPPT controller. The system load always runs from VSYS; the cell supplies only the shortfall.

7.3.2 Temperature-Qualified Charging

A 10 kΩ NTC against the cell stops charging outside about 0 to 50 °C. The ventilated roof keeps the cell well below the hot limit: 35 °C modelled on a calm August day, 37 °C measured in the field.

7.3.3 Low-Power Sensing

The soil probe is powered only while sampling. The battery divider uses a high-side switch that leaks 3 nA when off. The rain input is filtered in hardware (τ 0.22 ms on close, 22 ms on release) and counted by interrupt while the MCU sleeps.

8.2Detailed Installation Procedure

  1. Mount the bracket on a 25–50 mm pole, box 1.5–2 m above ground. Keep it 3 m or more from metal roofs and water tanks to limit RF shadow.
  2. Fit the probe cables through the PG7 glands with a drip loop below each gland, then torque the gland domes to 2.5 N·m.
  3. Insert a protected 18650 (ships at about 30 % charge), close the lid and torque the M4 screws to 1.2 N·m in a cross pattern.
  4. Tap the magnet on the lid logo. The LED blinks while joining and holds solid for 2 s when joined.
  5. Check the first uplinks in the network server: RSSI above −115 dBm at SF9 gives about 10 dB of fade margin.

9Power Supply Recommendations

  • Panel: 5 V class (10 series cells), VOC ≤ 6.5 V at 0 °C, 0.5–5 W. A 6 V class panel works, but wastes 10–20 % of its power, because input regulation holds it at 4.5 V.
  • Cell: protected 18650 Li-ion only (PCM cut-off ≥ 2.5 V). Unprotected cells can be deep-discharged after very long dark periods.
  • Energy margin: on the worst simulated day in Honolulu the panel still delivers 158 mAh, 14× the 1-minute-mode use. With no sun at all, a full cell lasts 251 days in rapid mode and about 551 days at 5 minutes.
  • Storage: ship and store at 30–50 % charge, 10–25 °C. The device stays joined to the network while stored, so expect one uplink per interval.

10Installation

10.1Guidelines

  • Antenna vertical, pointing down (as shipped), clear of the pole by 40 mm or more. Do not paint the antenna or the lid.
  • Radiation shield in free air: not over pavement or above a roof, and 1 m or more from walls.
  • Keep the panel unshaded from 9 am to 3 pm. In shade the device still runs for months on the cell, but charges slowly.
  • Coastal sites (within about 200 m of surf): use the stainless hardware kit and inspect the glands yearly. Salt-fog qualification is pending.

10.2Layout Example (PCBA)

The PCB is 4-layer, 1.6 mm (JLC 7628 stackup), with 50 Ω CPWG on L1 (0.35 mm trace, 0.20 mm gap) and a fenced RF path. The SHT40 sits on a slotted thermal island. KiCad 9 source, Gerbers and BOM: github.com/binaryBeachLLC/tradewind.
